# Buy the Gold, Pull the Feeds, Skip the Rips

The cheapest, cleanest, and highest-quality way to feed every stage of *deephouse* is to invert the obvious plan: instead of ripping SoundCloud and YouTube for bulk audio and then buying tracks to fill gaps, **buy professionally mixed compilations that ship their unmixed source tracks in the same lossless purchase** (fabric presents, Global Underground, Balance, Toolroom, DJ-Kicks), **pull DJ radio shows from podcast RSS feeds that serve the publisher's original 192–320 kbps MP3s**, and treat stream-ripping as a bounded, last-resort tool. Stream-ripping is both the lowest-quality route (SoundCloud free streams top out at **160 kbps AAC**, YouTube at **~130–160 kbps Opus / 128 kbps AAC**) and the highest-risk one under the 2025 *Bartz v. Anthropic* ruling, which blessed training on lawfully purchased copies and condemned libraries built from unauthorized downloads. Roughly **50 compilations cost $600–1,500**, yield **~1,250 professionally executed transitions with exact source audio** (the "gold" the miner needs) and simultaneously deliver **roughly 1,300 full-length, in-genre, lossless tracks (up to ~2,500 if Toolroom-heavy)** that *are* the mixing library. Free ground truth exists for key (Faraldo's Zenodo sets, audio included), beats/structure (Raveform, TISMIR 2026), cue points (EDM-CUE) and alignment (UnmixDB), and MixesDB — widely reported dead in 2024 — is back online with a MediaWiki API and 370,131 mix pages. Crowd-reaction mining is the weakest link in the whole plan: only ~20% of SoundCloud "drop" comments land on the drop, the YouTube heatmap resolves to ~72-second bins on a two-hour set, and every platform's API terms forbid retaining or training on that data. The sections below give the numbers, the terms, a three-tier budget, and the decisions only Charlie can make.

## 1. Executive summary

**SoundCloud rips are 160 kbps AAC at best, and there is a better free route.** SoundCloud's own help page states the standard streaming format is AAC, that free listeners get **96k and 160k** transcodes and only Go+ subscribers get **256k**, and that a 256k transcode exists only when the uploader supplied a ≥256k source ([SoundCloud Help, Audio Streaming Formats](https://help.soundcloud.com/hc/en-us/articles/53385714099611-Audio-Streaming-Formats)). In a 2026-10-07 sample of the 40 most recent uploads on 11 set-heavy accounts (Boiler Room, Keinemusik, Solomun, Anjunadeep, Toolroom and others), **not one upload exposed an uploader-enabled original-file download** ([Boiler Room SoundCloud](https://soundcloud.com/platform/tracks); [Keinemusik](https://soundcloud.com/keinemusik/tracks)). Meanwhile the same shows, when they run a podcast feed, serve the original MP3 as a plain RSS enclosure: **Hernán Cattáneo's Resident (809 episodes) and DHA FM (500) at 320 kbps, Toolroom Radio at 191 kbps median (the current two-hour episode is 320), Defected Radio at 192 kbps median** ([Cattáneo feed](https://podcast.hernancattaneo.com/feed.xml); [Toolroom Radio feed](https://portal-api.thisisdistorted.com/xml/toolroom-radio); [Defected feed](https://portal-api.thisisdistorted.com/xml/defected-in-the-house)). About **6,000 hours** of mixes sit behind such feeds, roughly 3,500 of them squarely deep/melodic/tech house.

**Gold is purchasable, plentiful, and doubles as the mixing library.** Digital editions of fabric presents (e.g. Carlita: 13 full tracks + a 61:23 continuous mix), Global Underground (GU48 Guy J: 27 FLAC tracks + two ~78-minute mixes), Balance 031 (28 FLAC tracks + a 155-minute mix), Toolroom Ibiza 2023 (50 WAV tracks + two mixes) and DJ-Kicks Honey Dijon (16 tracks + an 83-minute mix) each bundle unmixed sources and the continuous mix in one lossless purchase ([Discogs r37641903](https://www.discogs.com/release/37641903); [Discogs r36983247](https://www.discogs.com/release/36983247); [Discogs r27332082](https://www.discogs.com/release/27332082); [Discogs r27652005](https://www.discogs.com/release/27652005); [Discogs r35668738](https://www.discogs.com/release/35668738)). **Anjunadeep does not**: its yearly comps are sold as "(Mixed)" segments plus continuous mixes, with originals bought separately ([Anjunadeep store](https://anjunadeep.com/products/568658-anjunadeep-16)). At least 70 in-scope releases exist; 50 of them cost an estimated $600–1,500 and yield ~75–125 hours of mix and ~1,250 transitions.

**The store landscape changed in 2026.** Juno Download closed on 1 June 2026 ([Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/juno-download-shuts-down-after-20-years/)); Beatsource merged into Beatport from 3 March 2026 ([Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/1291036-beatport-and-beatsource-to-unite-into-one-premium-dj-platform)). Beatport sells MP3 at **$1.49 / $1.69 / $2.49** with a flat **+$0.70** for WAV/AIFF ([Beatport Deep House Top 100](https://www.beatport.com/genre/deep-house/12/top-100); [Beatport Support](https://support.beatport.com/hc/en-us/articles/8980748912020-Upgrading-Purchased-tracks-from-MP3-to-Lossless-Beatport-Store-Only)); Bandcamp and Volumo charge nothing extra for lossless ([Bandcamp Help](https://get.bandcamp.help/hc/en-us/articles/23020648367255); [Crossfader on Volumo](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/volumo-review/)). Beatport Streaming (ex-LINK) files are locked inside DJ software and useless for a custom pipeline ([Beatport Support](https://support.beatport.com/hc/en-us/articles/10922320713236-Will-my-offline-locker-be-deleted-if-I-cancel-my-subscription)).

**MixesDB is alive, and Raveform/EDM-CUE give EDM ground truth for free.** MixesDB announced closure for June 2024, was rescued as Mixes.wiki, and on 2026-10-07 answers at mixesdb.com on MediaWiki 1.46.1 with a working `api.php`, **370,131 mix pages, 150,604 complete tracklists, 31,597 Deep House and 68,024 Tech House pages**, and a robots.txt `Crawl-delay: 4` ([MixesDB main page](https://www.mixesdb.com/w/Main_Page); [MixesDB categoryinfo](https://www.mixesdb.com/w/api.php?action=query&prop=categoryinfo&titles=Category:Deep_House|Category:Tech_House|Category:Melodic_Techno|Category:Melodic_House|Category:House|Category:Techno|Category:Minimal&format=json)). Raveform (TISMIR, April 2026) provides **4,902 mixes, 53,780 aligned transitions and 1,423 EDM tracks with corrected beats, downbeats, tempo and Intro/Buildup/Breakdown/Drop labels** ([TISMIR 288](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)); EDM-CUE provides **~21k human cue points on 4,710 tracks** ([HF disco-eth/edm-cue](https://huggingface.co/datasets/disco-eth/edm-cue/blob/main/README.md)). The GiantSteps tempo/key audio links are dead (Beatport LOFI previews return 404), but Faraldo's Zenodo key sets ship audio ([Zenodo 1101082](https://zenodo.org/record/1101082)).

**Legal posture turns on acquisition channel, not on intent.** Judge Alsup held that training on purchased books was "exceedingly transformative and was a fair use," but that building a library from pirated copies was not ("Every factor points against fair use") — the exposure Anthropic settled for $1.5B ([Order, Dkt. 231](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.434709/gov.uscourts.cand.434709.231.0_3.pdf); [AP](https://abcnews.com/Technology/wireStory/judge-approves-15b-anthropic-settlement-pirated-books-train-134949964)). Record pools are the wrong instrument even though their files are good: ZIPDJ's terms say content is "STRICTLY FOR PROMOTIONAL USE AS A PROFESSIONAL DJ" and "not for building personal music catalogs or databases" ([ZIPDJ ToS](https://www.zipdj.com/?p=33443)).

**Crowd reactions are a research question, not a reward signal yet.** Only **20%** of SoundCloud "drop" comments sat on the actual drop in Yadati et al.'s 100-track study ([ISMIR 2014](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000297.pdf)); the YouTube Most Replayed heatmap is 100 normalised bins obtainable only through yt-dlp's InnerTube extraction ([yt-dlp common.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/master/yt_dlp/extractor/common.py)); and SoundCloud's API terms forbid using User Content "to train or otherwise develop any artificial intelligence technology" ([SoundCloud API ToU](https://developers.soundcloud.com/docs/api/terms-of-use)). The gold compilations are a better implicit reward: every transition in them is a choice a professional made and a label paid to release.

## 2. What each pipeline stage needs from data

The table maps the design-document pipeline onto data needs. "Minimum quality" follows the measured robustness results in Section 4: beat tracking survives 64 kbps MP3, MFCC-class features need ~160 kbps, listening tests need lossless or verified 320.

| Stage | Data type | Minimum quality | Quantity | Ground truth available? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ingest + QA | Any audio with provenance (receipt, feed URL, video ID) | n/a — QA tags each file with codec, declared bitrate, measured bandwidth | Everything below | LAME/Opus lowpass tables (Section 4) as reference |
| Constant-tempo beat grid | Full tracks and mixes | Genuine ≥128 kbps (beat F>0.85 even at 64 kbps MP3, [Mauch & Ewert](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2013/paper/000145.pdf)) | Eval: hundreds of tracks | Raveform 1,423 corrected beats/downbeats/tempo (YouTube-ID audio); EDM-CUE `beat_grid` on 4,710 tracks (Deezer IDs); UnmixDB `.beat.xml` + CC audio; Beat This! spectrograms for Harmonix/HJDB (no waveforms); GiantSteps tempo annotations (audio dead) |
| Beat-synchronous features, groove profile, key | Full tracks | ≥160 kbps MP3 / ≥128 kbps AAC-Opus for MFCC-class features; chroma more robust ([Urbano et al.](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000326.pdf)) | Eval: ~2,000 clips | Key: Beatport EDM Key 1,486 clips + GiantSteps+ 600 clips, audio included, CC BY-SA 4.0 ([Zenodo 1101082](https://zenodo.org/record/1101082); [Zenodo 1095691](https://zenodo.org/record/1095691)); groove: none for house (Groove MIDI has no electronic style) |
| Sections + cues | Full tracks | ≥160 kbps | Eval: ~1,400 tracks | Raveform Intro/Buildup/Breakdown/Drop/Cooldown/Bridge/Outro labels on 1,423 tracks; EDM-CUE 21k cue points (phrase-level); Harmonix 912 pop tracks |
| Renderer (mixing library) | Full-length extended/original mixes, 6–10 min | Lossless or verified 320/V0 — renders go through time-stretch and EQ, and lossy artefacts "are more noticeable" after tempo change ([Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/798615-what-is-the-best-audio-format-for-djs)) | 1,000–5,000 tracks | Store BPM/key as weak labels; the gold compilations' unmixed tracks supply ~1,300 of these (more if Toolroom-heavy) |
| Search L0/L1 | Library features + behavioural priors | n/a | n/a | Kim 2020: 86.1% of tempo changes <5%, 94.5% <10%; 2.5% of plays transposed; transition lengths peak every 32 beats ([Kim et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2008.10267)) |
| Miner — gold | Continuous mix + every unmixed source, same master | Lossless (alignment and automation recovery divide spectra) | 50 releases ≈ 75–125 h, ~1,250 transitions | Exact segment boundaries from release tracklists; UnmixDB validates the aligner with synthetic exact truth |
| Miner — silver | DJ sets + tracklists (boundaries only, sources not owned) | ≥160 kbps | Thousands of hours | MixesDB tracklists (timestamps rare: 160 of 31,597 Deep House pages carry `[0:00]`-style stamps); Raveform alignments; mixotic set (10 real mixes with track borders) |
| Learned critic | Gold + silver transitions, codec-matched | Any genuine ≥128 kbps if codec is logged and balanced | ~1,250 gold + silver at scale | Human preference ratings you collect; Chen et al. 2022 listening-test design (136 raters, α=0.799) as template ([arXiv 2110.06525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06525)) |
| Reaction mining (reward) | Timestamped comments, replay heatmaps, room-mic audio | n/a | Video-series subset only | None; Yadati 20% precision; Duico et al. heatmaps barely predictable ([arXiv 2309.06102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06102)) |
| Self-supervised pretraining | Long-form mixes | Any genuine ≥128 kbps, codec logged | ~10,000 h = 0.72 TB @160k, 1.44 TB @320k, 1.15 TB as 16 kHz mono PCM | None needed; MTG-Jamendo (55.7k CC tracks, 508 GB) is the only licence-clean full-track corpus ([MTG-Jamendo](https://github.com/MTG/mtg-jamendo-dataset)) |
| Stems (if used in blends) | Full tracks | Lossless/320 preferred; separation on ≤160 kbps is unvalidated | n/a | MUSDB18-HQ, MoisesDB, Slakh2100 — none EDM-specific; htdemucs_ft 9.0 dB SDR floor ([demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)) |

## 3. Sources, ranked, per need

### (a) Mixing-library tracks: Bandcamp and Volumo for lossless, Beatport for depth, pools for nothing

**Bandcamp** is the only store where lossless is free by design: buyers choose any of MP3 V0, MP3 320, FLAC, AAC, Ogg, ALAC, WAV or AIFF at one artist-set price ([Bandcamp Help, 12 Jun 2026](https://get.bandcamp.help/hc/en-us/articles/23020648367255)), Florida is not among the 31 states where Bandcamp collects sales tax on digital items ([Bandcamp Help, taxes](https://get.bandcamp.help/en/articles/15263230-what-about-taxes)), and the unofficial `bandcamp-dl` pulls an entire purchased collection as FLAC in one run ([iliana/bandcamp-dl](https://github.com/iliana/bandcamp-dl)). Its weaknesses are real: no cross-label cart, and every label page returned a bot challenge to automated fetching, so which deep-house labels maintain stores there is unverified. Because every purchase includes both FLAC and MP3-320 of the same master, Bandcamp is also where the project's lossy-vs-lossless A/B material should come from. Terms license content "solely for personal, non-commercial use" ([Bandcamp ToU mirror](https://toolongdidnotread.it/companies/bandcamp/terms/v/20260729_rev01)).

**Volumo** prices tracks at roughly €1.49 with MP3, WAV, AIFF and FLAC at the same price and a credit system giving 5–30% off pre-loaded funds; Digital DJ Tips calls it "the closest in spirit to Juno" ([Crossfader](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/volumo-review/); [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/juno-download-shuts-down-after-20-years/)). That makes it potentially the cheapest lossless store at **~$1.15–1.55 per track**, but its catalogue depth for melodic/tech house is unverified.

**Beatport** has the deepest melodic/tech-house catalogue and the clearest pricing: $1.49 catalogue, $1.69 new, $2.49 exclusive, plus 0.70 local-currency units for WAV or AIFF (blended ≈ $1.70 MP3 / $2.40 lossless), with multiple named carts for batching ([Beatport Support, carts](https://support.beatport.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412527201556-How-do-I-use-use-multiple-custom-carts)). Beatport recommends AIFF or FLAC over WAV because WAV carries no metadata ([Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/798615-what-is-the-best-audio-format-for-djs)). Two caveats: the MP3 bitrate (believed 320) is not stated on any fetched page, and there is no verified "Extended Mix" filter — though 12 of the Deep House Top 100 titles end in "Extended Mix" and underground "Original Mix" releases are already 6–8-minute DJ versions. API v4 has no normal developer sign-up; the community `beets-beatport4` plugin works by scraping the public OAuth client ID and logging in with a user password ([Samik081/beets-beatport4](https://github.com/Samik081/beets-beatport4)). Beatport's purchase licence says files can be listened to and burned but not traded ([Beatport Support](https://support.beatport.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412288508052-What-can-I-do-with-the-tracks-I-purchased-from-Beatport)).

**Traxsource** is the house specialist (Deep, Soulful, Afro, Jackin, Tech, Melodic/Progressive, Minimal/Deep Tech) at $1.49 / $1.99 / $2.49 in MP3, WAV and AIFF, and uniquely carries **Acapella, Efx/DJ Tools and STEMS** categories — a small but real source of artefact-free vocal/instrumental material ([Traxsource Deep House Top 100](https://www.traxsource.com/genre/13/deep-house/top); [Traxsource Acapella](https://www.traxsource.com/genre/7/acapella)). Whether lossless costs extra could not be confirmed (release pages returned 502/403).

**Record pools** deliver thousands of 320 kbps MP3s for $10–50 a month — Digital DJ Pool at $9.99/mo for indie house/deep/tech/afro ([Digital DJ Pool](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/record-pool-options-2026/)), ZIPDJ Pro Unlimited at $50/mo with a "Melodic House & Techno" bucket ([ZIPDJ pricing](https://www.zipdj.com/pricing)) — but the ZIPDJ terms forbid exactly this use: "not for building personal music catalogs or databases," no automated downloading, termination for violating the "letter or spirit" of the agreement ([ZIPDJ ToS](https://www.zipdj.com/?p=33443)). Free download gates (Hypeddit) deliver whatever bitrate the artist uploaded, one track at a time ([Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/reviews/hypeddit/)). Store previews are 2-minute "lo-fi" clips from the middle of the track and are useful only for metadata sanity checks ([Proton Help](https://intercom.help/proton-radio/en/articles/4474941-beatport-2-minute-previews-how-to-control-them-as-a-label-artist-or-dj); [stream.beatport.com](https://stream.beatport.com/)). 7digital, Qobuz and HDtracks are album/hi-res oriented and weak for DJ house ([Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/?p=2774639)).

| Route | Per track | 1,000 tracks | Format / licence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beatport MP3 (blended) | ~$1.70 | ~$1,700 | MP3 (bitrate unverified); personal use |
| Beatport WAV/AIFF | ~$2.40 | ~$2,400 | 16-bit lossless; personal use |
| Volumo lossless (with credit discount) | ~$1.15–1.55 | ~$1,150–1,550 | FLAC/WAV/AIFF at MP3 price; catalogue unverified |
| Bandcamp | artist-set; EP bundles often cheaper | n/a | All formats incl. FLAC; "personal, non-commercial use" |
| Gold compilations (Section 3c) | ~$0.45–1.15 effective per unmixed track | included in $600–1,500 for 50 releases | Lossless; also yields the transitions |
| Record pool (1–3 months) | ~$0.01–0.15 | $10–145 | 320 MP3; promotional licence forbids catalog-building |

### (b) DJ-set corpus: RSS enclosures first, YouTube for volume, SoundCloud only where nothing else exists

**Podcast RSS feeds (legal Tier 2, best quality).** Fifteen feeds were parsed on 2026-10-07 and bitrates computed from enclosure length ÷ duration. The This Is Distorted network serves ~192 kbps CBR: Defected Radio/Glitterbox **1,062 items since 2015, median 60 min, 192 kbps (min 128)**; Toolroom Radio **238 items, median 120 min, 191 kbps (152–329; TR862 verified at 288,002,126 bytes for 2 h = 320 kbps)**; Nora En Pure Purified **527 items @192k**; Glasgow Underground Radio **178 @191k**; Clapcast **585 @323k** ([Defected feed](https://portal-api.thisisdistorted.com/xml/defected-in-the-house); [Toolroom feed](https://portal-api.thisisdistorted.com/xml/toolroom-radio); [Purified feed](http://portal-api.thisisdistorted.com/xml/nora-en-pure-purified-radio); [Glasgow Underground feed](https://portal-api.thisisdistorted.com/xml/glasgow-underground-radio); [Clapcast feed](https://portal-api.thisisdistorted.com/xml/clapcast)). Podbean/Podtoo/SoundCloud-backed feeds serve the original file: **Hernán Cattáneo Resident 809 items @320k (ep. 804 verified 151,081,248 bytes)**, **Crosstown Mix Show 100 @320k, median 90 min**, **Yotto Odd One Out 104 @320k**, **DHA FM 500 @320k**, **Tomorrowland Friendship Mix 271 @~300–320k**, **Atish 113 @320k**, **RA Podcast 500 items (variable; RA.1059 is 195 kbps VBR at 48 kHz)**, Joris Voorn Spectrum 100 @209k ([Cattáneo](https://podcast.hernancattaneo.com/feed.xml); [Crosstown](https://feed.podbean.com/thecrosstownmixshow/feed.xml); [Yotto](https://feed.podtoo.com/distro/jFBUMUI_); [DHA](https://feeds.soundcloud.com/users/soundcloud:users:15904471/sounds.rss); [Friendship Mix](https://feeds.soundcloud.com/users/soundcloud:users:243635960/sounds.rss); [Atish](https://feeds.feedburner.com/atishmusic); [RA](https://ra.co/xml/podcast.xml); [Spectrum](https://jorisvoornspectrum.libsyn.com/rss)). Avoid Simplecast-hosted feeds, which transcode to 128 kbps (Sultan + Shepard Dialekt, 351 items), and note Miss Monique's feed is 128 kbps because of the upload ([Dialekt feed](https://feeds.simplecast.com/3dnPjov_); [Miss Monique feed](https://feeds.soundcloud.com/users/soundcloud:users:17716472/sounds.rss)). SoundCloud-generated feeds cap at **500 items** (RA shows 500 while Apple lists 523; DHA 500 vs 598) ([hearthis.at FAQ](https://hearthis.at/faq/)). Discover feeds with the keyless iTunes Search API and bulk-fetch with `podcast-dl` 12.1.4 ([iTunes Search](https://itunes.apple.com/search?media=podcast&limit=4&term=Toolroom%20Radio); [npm podcast-dl](https://registry.npmjs.org/podcast-dl)). These are studio/radio mixes: clean desk feeds with voice-over IDs to trim, no crowd. Not on any feed: Anjunadeep Edition, Solomun +1, Keinemusik, Paradise, Transitions, This Never Happened, Innervisions, Afterlife.

**YouTube video series (Tier 3, volume).** Ten channels hold **~30,100 hours of ≥45-minute set video**: HÖR 9,210 h (8,743 streams, median 55 min), Boiler Room 6,364 h, Beatport 3,462 h, Defected 2,550 h, Mixmag 2,548 h, DJ Mag 2,114 h, Tomorrowland 2,004 h, Anjunadeep 1,181 h (424 of 427 stream entries are long-form, Edition episode ~613 by Oct 2026), RA 355 h, Cercle 271 h — measured via `yt-dlp --flat-playlist` on 2026-10-07 ([HÖR streams](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCmfF7JZv26UUKyRedViGIlw/streams); [Boiler Room](https://www.youtube.com/@boilerroom/videos); [Beatport streams](https://www.youtube.com/@beatport/streams); [Anjunadeep streams](https://www.youtube.com/@Anjunadeep/streams); [Cercle](https://www.youtube.com/@Cercle/videos)). Title keywords are a poor genre proxy (only 177 of ~9,900 Boiler Room titles contain house/deep/melodic/tech), so an audio classifier is needed; a plausible in-genre yield is 8–12k hours before dedup. Delivered audio is itag 251 Opus (~130–160 kbps class) or 140 AAC 128 kbps; 256k formats are Premium-only and mostly YouTube Music ([yt-dlp issue 9724](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/issues/9724)). From a datacenter IP, YouTube returned HTTP 429 after ~30k flat entries and refused media with "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot"; yt-dlp 2026.08 also wants a JavaScript runtime and, on flagged IPs, PO tokens via `bgutil-ytdlp-pot-provider` 2.0.1, which "does not guarantee bypassing 403 errors or bot checks" ([yt-dlp EJS wiki](https://github.com/yt-dlp/yt-dlp/wiki/EJS); [PyPI POT provider](https://pypi.org/project/bgutil-ytdlp-pot-provider)). Boiler Room's official audio archive now lives on Apple Music as a streaming catalogue split into per-track items for royalty payment — unusable as a corpus and, by splitting, useless for transition mining ([RA News](https://ra.co/news/73300)). On crowd audio, the published record is thin: Boiler Room produces "a separate dedicated audio mix" plus a multitrack ([Broadcast Now](https://www.broadcastnow.co.uk/beats-international-live-streaming-music/5101198.article)), Ultra's broadcaster "relies heavily on ambience microphones" (same source), HÖR's small studio chain is undocumented ([ADAM Audio](https://www.adam-audio.com/blog/hoer-berlin/)). Crowd presence must be tested empirically per series (download the final ~90 s of a few sets and measure post-music level).

**SoundCloud accounts (Tier 3, lowest quality).** Set-dense accounts: Boiler Room `platform` 8,696 tracks (35 of 40 recent ≥45 min), Keinemusik 433 (38/40), Anjunadeep 2,740 (38/40), Solomun 117 (29/40), Toolroom 2,752 (22/40), Cercle 239 (16/40); big label accounts (Crosstown, Innervisions, Afterlife, All Day I Dream) are mostly 5–8-minute premieres and need `--match-filter "duration>2700"` ([Solomun](https://soundcloud.com/solomun/tracks); [Anjunadeep](https://soundcloud.com/anjunadeep/tracks)). yt-dlp offers `download` (original file) only when the uploader enables it, otherwise `hls_aac_160k`, and 256k AAC only with a Go+ `oauth_token` cookie — and no yt-dlp test verifies that 256k actually returns ([SoundCloud API blog](https://developers.soundcloud.com/blog/api-streaming-urls)). SoundCloud user pages need `curl_cffi` impersonation; three accounts returned zero entries without it. Rule: whenever a SoundCloud show has an RSS twin, take the RSS enclosure.

**Other hosts.** hearthis.at transcodes to 192 kbps CBR, has per-user `podcast.xml` feeds and a "Live DJ Sets" section, but its in-genre volume is small ([hearthis.at FAQ](https://hearthis.at/faq/)). Mixcloud's standard on-demand stream is 64 kbps AAC+, with ≥160 kbps Opus only for shows uploaded by Pro members since 2022 (per Mixcloud's own "HQ Audio" feature post, verified by the coordinator), and its terms bar downloading outright, so it is a last resort ([Mixcloud Terms](https://www.mixcloud.com/terms/)). Twitch's DJ Program licenses live performance only; VODs and clips with program music "will continue to be muted or removed" ([aidjsets.com](https://aidjsets.com/blog/twitch-s-dj-program-how-to-legally-stream-music-in-october-2025)). Discovery helpers: `fetch-mix-cli` (Aug 2026) resolves tracklists via MixesDB's API, WordPress mirrors and yt-dlp comments ([fetch-mix-cli](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/alexgorbatchev/fetch-mix-cli)); watchthedj.com tags HÖR sets by genre ([watchthedj](https://watchthedj.com/channels/hoer-berlin)).

### (c) Gold mixed + unmixed compilations: the named releases

The Discogs format tag is the quick filter — a digital edition tagged both "File, Compilation" and "File, Mixed" bundles unmixed tracks and the continuous mix. The in-scope pool is at least 70 releases.

**fabric presents (2019–2026).** Digital editions of most titles are unmixed full-length tracks plus one continuous mix, but the fabric store sells digital as mixed segments (for Mind Against: "18 mixed track MP3s that when played in sequence form the continuous mix," £8 WAV or MP3, no FLAC) and sends buyers of individual tracks to the **artist's own Bandcamp** ([store.fabriclondon.com](https://store.fabriclondon.com/products/fabric-presents-mind-against)). ### (f) Identification and alignment tools, and the tempo change each tolerates

Real DJ behaviour sets the requirement: **86.1% of tempo adjustments are under 5%, 94.5% under 10%, 98.6% under 20%, and only 2.5% of plays are transposed** ([Kim et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2008.10267)); Sonnleitner & Widmer found no DJ-set cases exceeding 7% ([DAFx-14](https://www.dafx14.fau.de/papers/dafx14_reinhard_sonnleitner_quad_based_audio_fingerpr.pdf)).

| Tool / method | Tolerance | Measured on real techno/house mixes (Sonnleitner 2016, accuracy) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| audfprint (Shazam-style landmarks) | Breaks beyond ~3% pitch shift; robust to pure time-stretch but "unable to cope with time-scale modification" (speed) | 0.637 | Python; licence unverified |
| Panako (Six & Leman) | Pitch/time modifications up to ~8–10%; performance "decreases with time-scale modification of more than eight percent" | 0.360 | Java, AGPL; bundles Olaf |
| Qfp quad-based (Sonnleitner) | ±30% speed/tempo; "near perfect" within 90–115% speed and 80–120% tempo | **0.876** | Code availability unconfirmed |
| Chromaprint/AcoustID | Exact recordings, whole-file | not evaluated | Identify owned source files only |
| Beat-synchronous subsequence DTW (Kim 2020) | Tempo-invariant by construction; key-invariant 12-shift variant; match-rate threshold 0.4 | used for Raveform's 53,780 transitions | Requires known candidate tracks; yields the warping path and cue-in/out |

Sources: [Panako ISMIR 2014](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2014/paper/000122.pdf); [Sonnleitner et al. ISMIR 2016](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/2/2294/files/2016/07/187_Paper.pdf); [Kim et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2008.10267). The design implication: for the **gold miner** (sources known), use beat-synchronous DTW and skip fingerprinting entirely; for **silver** (sources unknown), a Qfp-class or ACRCloud derivative-works identifier is needed, because audfprint/Chromaprint will miss pitched tracks and Panako scored worst on real mixes.

Two further measured facts shape policy. A model trained on clean audio lost **57.1%** relative accuracy on 32 kbps MP3 and 9.6% on 64 kbps AAC-LC; retraining with coded audio recovered an average **+24.1%** across seven codec conditions with no loss on clean audio, but transfer to unseen codecs was small (Opus +1.9%) ([Rao & Peters, arXiv 2112.04841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04841)). And spectral cutoff is "**neither necessary nor sufficient**" as a lossy detector: a naive classifier that latched onto the cutoff lost ~70 points on AAC when cutoffs were varied, genuine old recordings can roll off at 16 kHz, and "quiet, sparse lossless tracks … were misclassified as lossy" ([arXiv 2407.21545](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21545v1); [Well-Tempered Computer](https://thewelltemperedcomputer.com/SW/AudioTools/Detect.htm)). Transcodes are common enough to matter: an audit of a 3,400-file DJ library found **214 (~6%) fake lossless files**, some from store purchases ([DJ TechTools forum](https://forum.djtechtools.com/t/i-stop-playing-fake-wavs-at-gigs-heres-how-to-audit-your-entire-library-in-minutes/154703)). No study was found measuring Demucs-class separation on MP3/AAC/Opus inputs, and none measuring pre-echo's effect on kick-onset timing; both are gaps the project should close with its own A/B on Bandcamp FLAC-vs-320 pairs.

### Recommended ingest QA policy and the codec-matching rule

Every file, regardless of source, passes one ingest routine: read declared codec, bitrate and sample rate with `ffprobe`; decode to mono float; compute the long-term average spectrum over the loudest 25% of frames; define effective bandwidth as the highest bin within ~40–50 dB of the 1–5 kHz median; compare against the table above; and tag the file `{codec, declared_bitrate, est_bandwidth, transcode_suspect, provenance}`. A declared 320 with a 16–17 kHz shelf is a transcode; a FLAC with a 19–20 kHz shelf is fake lossless; a 20 kHz shelf in an Opus or `.webm` file is normal. Because cutoff is only a screen, confirm suspects with a second cue (speckled MP3 "holes" above the shelf, or the LAME lowpass taper). Transcode-suspect files are excluded from listening tests and from the lossless tier but kept, labelled, for mining. Provenance is recorded alongside because it is also the legal variable.

The critic rule follows directly from the shortcut-learning result: if real transitions are 160–192 kbps rips or feeds and renders are lossless, a critic can separate "real" from "rendered" by bandwidth alone. So **re-encode every render through the corpus's codec/bitrate distribution before scoring**, log codec as metadata on every training example and balance it across classes, and codec-augment the real examples with the codecs actually present (MP3, AAC-LC, Opus) rather than one, since augmentation transfers poorly to unseen codecs.

## 5. Legal and terms posture

**Case law and policy in a few sentences.** In *Bartz v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal., 23 June 2025) Judge Alsup held that training on books was "exceedingly transformative and was a fair use," that digitising print copies Anthropic "purchased … fair and square" was fair use, but that "Anthropic had no entitlement to use pirated copies for its central library" and that later purchase "will not absolve it of liability for the theft" ([Order, Dkt. 231](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.434709/gov.uscourts.cand.434709.231.0_3.pdf)); Anthropic settled the piracy claims for **$1.5 billion**, approved July 2026 ([AP](https://abcnews.com/Technology/wireStory/judge-approves-15b-anthropic-settlement-pirated-books-train-134949964)). In *Kadrey v. Meta* (25 June 2025) Judge Chhabria also found fair use on that record but warned that "in many circumstances it will be illegal to copy copyright-protected works" to train generative AI, flagged "market dilution" as a potentially heavy factor, and left Meta's torrenting/distribution claim for a Feb 2027 hearing ([Order, Dkt. 598](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.415175/gov.uscourts.cand.415175.598.0.pdf); [scheduling](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/03/27/kadrey-v-meta-gets-scheduling-order-summary-judgment-motion-hearing-for-distribution-claim-wont-be-heard-until-feb-25-2027/)). The Copyright Office's May 2025 Part 3 report places "noncommercial research or analysis that doesn't enable reproduction of protected content" at the most-likely-fair end, says unlawful access weighs against fair use, and notes "'publicly available' is not synonymous with 'authorized'" ([Lowenstein](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/us-copyright-office-releases-long-awaited-report-on-generative-ai-training-and-copyright-law); [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Copyright-Office-Issues-Key-Guidance-on-Fair-Use-in-Generative-AI-Training)). No court has ruled on music training; the UMG v. Suno/Udio complaints now allege YouTube stream-ripping, and Suno/Udio filed an amicus in the still-pending *Yout v. RIAA* §1201 appeal because a ruling that YouTube's "rolling cipher" is an access control would be used against them ([TorrentFreak](https://torrentfreak.com/suno-udio-wade-into-youtube-ripper-circumvention-lawsuit-appeal-251008/)). There is no US statutory text-and-data-mining exception; the §1201 TDM exemption covers literary works and films only ([Berkeley Law](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/case-project/1201-exemption-text-data-mining/)).

**Platform terms, quoted.** SoundCloud (amended 17 Aug 2026): "You must not copy, rip or capture, or attempt to copy, rip or capture, any Content from the Platform"; "You must not employ scraping or similar techniques"; content may not be used "for the purposes of informing, training developing (or as input to) artificial intelligence technologies" without rightsholder authorisation; enforcement "may include taking court action" ([SoundCloud ToU](https://soundcloud.com/terms-of-use)). YouTube (15 Dec 2023): users may not "access, reproduce, download, distribute … or otherwise use any part of the Service or any Content except: (a) as expressly authorized by the Service," nor use "automated means (such as robots, botnets or scrapers)" ([YouTube Terms](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms)). Mixcloud: "downloading, side-loading" are "strictly prohibited"; §5.1.16 bans "Training any machine learning or AI model using data or content from the Platform"; §11.3 reserves rights against text and data mining ([Mixcloud Terms](https://www.mixcloud.com/terms/)). Bandcamp (7 May 2026): content licensed "solely for personal, non-commercial use" ([Bandcamp ToU mirror](https://toolongdidnotread.it/companies/bandcamp/terms/v/20260729_rev01)). ## 6. Recommended acquisition plan with budget tiers

The plan rests on one arithmetic fact: the gold compilations are not a separate line item from the mixing library — **50 releases × 13–50 unmixed tracks ≈ 1,300 full-length, lossless, in-genre tracks for a balanced series mix (up to ~2,500 if Toolroom-heavy)**, which is the renderer's library, plus ~1,250 transitions the miner can align exactly. Buying them first therefore funds two pipeline stages at once, while bulk Beatport buying funds only one at ~$2.40 per lossless track.

### $0 tier: enough to build and validate every stage except the critic

Pull the podcast feeds (≈ 6,000 h, ~0.5–0.9 TB at 192–320 kbps; the ~3,500 in-genre hours are ~0.3–0.5 TB), starting with Cattáneo, DHA, Toolroom, Crosstown, Yotto and Defected. Download the free ground truth: Beatport EDM Key + GiantSteps+ (2.9 GB, audio included), UnmixDB (4.2 GB, exact beat and cue truth), the mixotic set (1 GB of mixes + 9.4 GB of CC reference tracks — a free techno/house mini-library for pipeline development), the Raveform zip (metadata, corrected beats, alignments), the EDM-CUE parquet (293 kB), and — only if a 50 fps mel frontend is acceptable — Beat This!'s harmonix.zip (45 GB) and hjdb subset. Mirror MixesDB's Deep House and Tech House categories through the API at the published 4-second delay (~4.6 days) for tracklists and genre labels. Fetch Discogs/MusicBrainz metadata for everything. For SSL pretraining without any ToS exposure, MTG-Jamendo's electronic-tagged subset (≥1,100 tracks; full set 508 GB) is the only licence-clean full-track corpus. This tier builds and benchmarks ingest, beat grid, key, structure and the aligner; it does not give in-genre gold.

### ~$500 tier: the first gold corpus and a codec A/B set

Buy roughly 20–25 releases: ~15 on-genre fabric presents via the artists' Bandcamp pages (≈ $13 each ≈ $200, Bandcamp price unverified), then GU48 Guy J, GU Select #11, GU Afterhours 10, Balance 031, DJ-Kicks Honey Dijon and Toolroom Ibiza 2023 (an assumed $15–40 each ≈ $150–250, prices unverified). Expected yield: **~400 transitions, ~30 hours of continuous mix, ~400–450 unmixed lossless tracks**, ~25–35 GB in FLAC. Where a release is on Bandcamp, download both FLAC and MP3-320 of the same master to run the lossless-vs-320 separation and onset-timing A/B. Spend the remainder (~$50–150) on 30–100 specific tracks from Bandcamp or Volumo that the renderer needs for test cases (e.g., long-intro deep house, vocal tech house). Skip Anjunadeep at this tier.

### ~$2,000 tier: fifty gold releases plus library breadth

Complete the gold corpus to ~50 releases (**$600–1,500**, estimate), adding the remaining GU numbered/Select/Afterhours/Adapt titles, the Balance Presents titles and Balance 032 (Discogs lists CD and vinyl; confirm a digital unmixed + CM edition before buying), Toolroom Miami/Ibiza/Amsterdam editions, Defected ITH Ibiza 2025 and 2026 (buy both the mixed and unmixed editions), house-leaning DJ-Kicks, Crosstown Spirits III/IV, and two or three Anjunadeep comps on the $17.49 CD+WAV route if the melodic sound is a priority (originals cost another ~$80 each). Expected yield: **~1,250 transitions, 75–125 h of mix, ~1,300 lossless tracks (a balanced mix of 15 fabric, 19 GU, 8 Balance, 4 Toolroom, 4 DJ-Kicks; up to ~2,500 if Toolroom-heavy)**, ~75–100 GB FLAC (~150–200 GB if WAV). Spend the remaining **$500–900** on library breadth: at Volumo's ~$1.15–1.55 lossless that is 350–750 tracks; at Beatport's ~$2.40 lossless, 200–375; at Beatport MP3 (~$1.70, bitrate unverified), 300–500. Use Beatport for melodic/tech-house labels absent elsewhere, Traxsource for deep/soulful and for its Acapella/STEMS categories, Bandcamp wherever the label has a store. Do not spend any of it on record pools, SoundCloud Go+ or Beatport Streaming.

### Storage

Compressed originals plus derived features for the full plan land at roughly **2–3 TB**: 10,000 h of mixes is 0.72 TB at 160 kbps, 0.86 TB at 192 kbps, 1.44 TB at 320 kbps, and 1.15 TB as 16 kHz mono PCM (derive, do not store both); 5,000 six-minute tracks are ~72 GB as 320 MP3, ~175–200 GB as FLAC, ~315 GB as WAV ([Beatportal file sizes](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/798615-what-is-the-best-audio-format-for-djs)); the gold corpus adds ~0.1–0.2 TB; the research datasets add ~0.2 TB (or ~0.7 TB with MTG-Jamendo's full 508 GB). A 4 TB working drive with checksummed backups of the purchased material (receipts stored alongside) is the right size.

### Order of operations against the pipeline

*Next two sessions (ingest → beat grid → features/key).* Get the $0-tier datasets and a seed of two or three gold releases (e.g. fabric presents Carlita, GU48, Balance 031: ~70 lossless tracks, four continuous mixes) plus three RSS feeds. Build the ingest QA routine on those (they span lossless, 320, 192). Benchmark the constant-tempo beat grid on UnmixDB's `.beat.xml` truth and the mixotic CC tracks (audio included, CC) before touching Raveform's YouTube-ID tracks; benchmark key on the two Zenodo sets against the EDMM 63.7% floor.

*Sessions three to five (sections+cues → renderer → L0/L1 search).* Structure on Raveform's 1,423 labelled tracks requires their YouTube audio — a Tier-3 decision (Decision 2) — so start with EDM-CUE cue points matched to owned tracks via Deezer ISRC, and hand-label sections on 50–100 owned gold tracks. Expand the gold corpus to the $500 tier so the renderer has 400+ lossless tracks and the search has Kim 2020's priors to score against.

*Later (miner → critic → SSL → reaction).* Align every gold continuous mix with beat-synchronous DTW, validated on UnmixDB; recover automation by spectral division. Mine silver boundaries from the RSS corpus using MixesDB tracklists and a Qfp-class or ACRCloud identifier. Only then decide the YouTube question for SSL volume and reaction mining, and run the crowd-presence test on a few sets per series before investing further.

## 7. Decisions for Charlie

1. **Lossless vs 320 for the mixing library.** Options: lossless everywhere; 320 everywhere; lossless for render/listening material and 320 for breadth. Trade-off: 320 is indistinguishable in blind listening (NPR 2015: 36.0% picked WAV vs 33.3% chance) and sufficient for every analysis stage, but the renderer time-stretches and EQs, where lossy artefacts grow, and listening-test raters must not be judging codec noise ([Digital DJ Pool](https://digitaldjpool.com/blog/mp3-vs-wav-for-djs/); [Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/798615-what-is-the-best-audio-format-for-djs)). Default: **lossless for everything the renderer touches** — it is free on Bandcamp/Volumo and comes with the gold comps anyway — and 320 only for breadth tracks used purely for features.

2. **Use yt-dlp on YouTube/SoundCloud at all?** Options: (a) never — RSS and purchases only; (b) bounded — a documented, residential-IP, cookie-authenticated fetch of a defined subset (Raveform's 1,423 annotated tracks for structure evaluation; one or two video series for the crowd-presence test and reaction pilot); (c) bulk harvest for 10k hours of SSL. Trade-off: (a) forfeits EDM structure ground truth and crowd signal; (c) is the lowest-quality audio, breaches both ToS, is the fact pattern labels are litigating, and will fight bot checks for weeks; (b) accepts a small, explainable ToS breach for data that has no substitute. Default: **(b) at most, decided per subset and logged** — never rip a SoundCloud set that has an RSS twin, and build SSL volume from RSS (~6,000 h) and MTG-Jamendo before considering YouTube.

3. **Subscribe to Digital DJ Pool for breadth?** Options: subscribe one to three months and pull thousands of 320 MP3s; use it for discovery only; skip. Trade-off: it is $9.99/month for indie house at 320 kbps, but pool licences are promotional and ZIPDJ's — the only pool terms readable — prohibit building catalogs or databases and automated downloading; Digital DJ Pool's own terms were not fetched. Default: **skip for the dataset**; if curiosity wins, use it for discovery and buy what you keep.

4. **Buy SoundCloud Go+ for 256k?** Options: subscribe and fetch 256k via an oauth cookie; do not. Trade-off: 256k only exists for uploads supplied at ≥256k, yt-dlp has no test confirming 256k is actually returned, and the subscription does not change the "copy, rip or capture" clause. Default: **no**; RSS twins already serve 192–320 kbps originals, and the subscription money buys lossless tracks instead.

5. **Codec-match the critic at 160/192k?** Options: train and score on whatever bitrate each file came in; re-encode renders to the corpus distribution and codec-augment; restrict the critic's corpus to ≥320/lossless (gold only). Trade-off: a mixed-bitrate corpus with lossless renders hands the critic a bandwidth shortcut; restricting to gold loses silver volume. Default: **yes — codec-match and augment**, with codec logged per example and balanced across classes, and the gold-only critic kept as the ablation baseline.

6. **Which gold series first?** Options: fabric presents, Global Underground, Balance, Toolroom, DJ-Kicks, Anjunadeep, Defected. Trade-off: fabric presents is cheapest per release and cleanest (artist Bandcamps, lossless, 13–20 full tracks) but gives fewer transitions each; GU gives 25–38 transitions and ~2.5 h per release and is the most on-genre at scale; Anjunadeep is the most melodic but sells segments only and costs ~$95 per comp for full source coverage. Default: **fabric presents (on-genre titles) → GU48/Select #11/Afterhours 10 → Balance 031 → Honey Dijon DJ-Kicks → Toolroom Ibiza 2023 → remaining GU → Defected ITH → Anjunadeep last**, verifying per release that the digital track count matches the CD tracklist (Chaos In The CBD shows it is not guaranteed).

7. **Crawl MixesDB or rely on Raveform's metadata?** Options: use Raveform's `mixes.jsonl`/alignments only; crawl MixesDB Deep House + Tech House (~100k pages, ~4.6 days at the 4-s delay); crawl everything (~17 days). Trade-off: Raveform is already cleaned and aligned but frozen (submitted June 2025) and techno-heavy; MixesDB is live with daily edits but its licence is undeclared and only a minority of tracklists carry timestamps. Default: **Raveform first, then an incremental MixesDB crawl of the two house categories for sets not in Raveform**, stored privately and never redistributed.

8. **How to treat crowd-reaction mining.** Options: build it as the reward model now; run a small pilot; drop it in favour of the gold corpus as implicit reward. Trade-off: the evidence is thin and discouraging — 20% of "drop" comments on the drop, Most Replayed near-unpredictable and ~72 s per bin on a two-hour set, no published confirmation of room mics on Boiler Room/Cercle/HÖR, and SoundCloud/YouTube/Reddit terms all forbid retaining or training on the data — while every gold transition is a professional's choice a label paid to release. Default: **gold-as-reward first; a pilot later** limited to the crowd-presence tail test and a heatmap-vs-transition correlation on one video series, treated as weak labels and documented as Tier 3.

9. **YouTube Data API vs the heatmap field.** Options: Data API comments under the Developer Policies; heatmap and comments via yt-dlp; neither. Trade-off: the Data API is authorised but requires deleting or refreshing stored data every 30 days and forbids derived metrics and aggregation beyond channel level — incompatible with a persistent reward dataset — and 30,000 videos cost ≥30,000 units (≥3 days at 10,000/day; ~60 days at 2,000 comments per video); the heatmap is not in the API at all and comes only from unauthorised page extraction. Default: **if Decision 8's pilot runs, take the heatmap plus top comments via yt-dlp for a few hundred videos and treat it as Tier 3**; do not architect anything on the Data API's retention rules.

10. **Which store mix for breadth after the gold.** Options: Beatport-centric; Bandcamp/Volumo-centric; Traxsource for house specialities. Trade-off: Beatport has the deepest melodic/tech catalogue but charges +$0.70 for lossless and has no sanctioned API; Bandcamp and Volumo include lossless but are bulk-unfriendly or unverified in depth; Traxsource uniquely sells acapellas and stems. Default: **Bandcamp where the label has a store, Volumo credits for bulk lossless, Beatport for gaps, Traxsource for acapellas/stems** — and audit every file at ingest regardless of store.

11. **Publishing renders.** Options: public demos on YouTube/SoundCloud; Mixcloud; private only; CC/self-produced demo material. Trade-off: publishing renders that contain the recordings is the top risk tier and surrenders the "no reproduction enabled" framing that favours the project. Default: **private renders for evaluation; any public demo rendered from CC or self-produced tracks, or uploaded to Mixcloud with a full tracklist.**
