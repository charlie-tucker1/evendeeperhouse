# Professionally mixed DJ compilations with purchasable unmixed source tracks ("gold" transition dataset), as of Oct 2026

Method note: Bandcamp (all subdomains), Beatport, Traxsource and discogs.com HTML are all behind bot challenges from this environment, so release structure (unmixed tracks vs. "(Mixed)" segments vs. "Continuous Mix" files, format, catalogue number, date) was verified through the public Discogs API (`api.discogs.com`), label web stores, and press. Discogs release IDs are given so the report writer / user can open `https://www.discogs.com/release/<id>` directly. Bandcamp prices could not be verified; the only verified digital prices are from the Anjuna and fabric stores.

Vocabulary used below:
- **Unmixed + CM**: one digital release containing the full-length unmixed source tracks *and* the continuous mix file(s) — the ideal "gold" product (exact boundaries, known sources, recoverable automation).
- **Mixed segments + CM**: the release contains track-by-track *cuts of the mix* ("(Mixed)" versions, i.e. the CD-indexed segments) plus continuous mix files; the unmixed originals must be bought separately as singles/EPs. Boundaries are still exact (segment lengths), but the source audio is a separate purchase.
- **Mixed segments only**: CD-style mixed cuts, no continuous file, no unmixed originals.

## Key Question 1 — Anjunadeep: do "Anjunadeep NN", Explorations, Reflections and artist mixes come as mixed + unmixed bundles? Where, formats, price, count?

### Takeaway
No Anjunadeep compilation is sold as a "mixed + unmixed" bundle. The yearly "Anjunadeep NN" digital release is **mixed segments (+ 2 continuous mixes on the own-store/Bandcamp edition)**; the unmixed originals are sold separately as singles/EPs/samplers on the same label. Explorations and Reflections are **unmixed** samplers (no mix), and I found no Anjunadeep-released artist DJ-mix compilations (Lane 8/Ben Böhmer/Yotto/Tinlicker/Eli & Fur/Marsh) in Discogs. There are 16 yearly "Anjunadeep NN" compilations (2009–2026).

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The Anjunadeep "gold" workflow is: buy the compilation (segments + continuous mixes; the "OD"/own-store and physical-bundled WAV edition carries the 2×~77-min continuous mixes, the Beatport-style "D" edition may not), then buy each source track as a single/EP (most are released as singles on Anjunadeep before or alongside the comp; Anjunadeep 16 vinyl lists 12 of 42 as extended mixes). Boundaries are exact from the segment durations; the segments themselves are already EQ'd/faded crossfade regions, which is useful for learning the *output* but the unmixed input must be matched separately.
- Expect ~36–40 transitions per yearly comp (38–42 segments across two ~77-min mixes), ~2.6 h of mixed audio per release; many segments are 1–3 min "Interlude Edits" (short overlaps / filler) so transition density is high but some segments are edits that do not exist as standalone singles (e.g. "Anjunadeep 16 Interlude Edit", "Outro Edit").
- Cost per release: $9.99 (MP3) or ~$17.49 (CD incl. WAV download + continuous mixes) at the Anjuna store; FLAC/WAV per-track pricing on the Anjuna music store is $1.99/track, i.e. ~$80 for 40 segments — so the CD+WAV-download route is the cheap lossless route. Unmixed singles add ~$1.5–2 per track (unverified typical single pricing), i.e. roughly $60–80 per comp for ~40 originals.

### Gaps
- Could not load anjunadeep.bandcamp.com (Bandcamp bot challenge), so Bandcamp price and whether the Bandcamp edition of Anjunadeep 16 includes the two continuous-mix files are unverified (Anjunadeep 15's own-store edition did, per Discogs).
- No evidence found of Anjunadeep-released, commercially sold artist DJ-mix compilations by Lane 8, Ben Böhmer, Yotto, Tinlicker, Eli & Fur or Marsh; Lane 8's "This Never Happened" and Anjunadeep Edition mixes are free streams (not verified here). Treat as "none found", not "none exist".
- Whether every "(Mixed)" segment has a purchasable unmixed original is release-specific (edits/interludes often do not); needs a per-release tracklist diff against the label's single catalogue.

## Key Question 2 — fabric: "fabric presents" (2019–2026) and legacy fabric/FABRICLIVE: are unmixed tracks included with digital purchases? Where and in what formats?

### Takeaway
Yes for most "fabric presents" titles: the digital edition is **unmixed full-length tracks + one continuous-mix file** (e.g. Carlita 13 + CM; DJ Tennis 20 + CM; Saoirse, Rødhåd, Bonobo, Octo Octa & Eris Drew, Overmono, MCDE, TSHA, Leon Vynehall, Helena Hauff, Red Axes etc. are all tagged "File, WAV/FLAC, Compilation + File, WAV/FLAC, Mixed" on Discogs). Important nuance: fabric's **own store** sells the digital as *mixed segments* (£8 WAV/MP3) and points buyers to **the artist's Bandcamp page** for the individual tracks; some titles (Chaos in the CBD, Mind Against "Exclusives") only include the *exclusive* tracks unmixed, not every track in the mix.

### Inferences
- For deep/melodic/tech-house relevance, the best fabric presents picks are: Bonobo, Kölsch (CM only on Discogs—check Bandcamp), Martinez Brothers, Octo Octa & Eris Drew, MCDE, Chaos In The CBD (partial), Saoirse, TSHA, Carlita, DJ Tennis, Red Axes, Floorplan, Pretty Girl, Salute, Mind Against (melodic techno). Techno/electro/dnb/pop titles (Amelie Lens, Helena Hauff, Rødhåd, Sama' Abdulhadi, Chase & Status, Pola & Bryson, Shygirl, Confidence Man, The Streets, Eliza Rose) are off-genre.
- Where to buy: the **artist's Bandcamp** album (pattern `<artist>.bandcamp.com/album/fabric-presents-<artist>`) is the place that bundles unmixed tracks + CM in lossless (Bandcamp sells FLAC/WAV/ALAC/AIFF by default); the fabric store sells only mixed segments (£8). Beatport also carries the WAV/FLAC "Compilation + Mixed" editions per Discogs tagging.
- Yield: ~13–20 tracks per mix → 12–19 transitions per release, ~60–80 min of mixed audio. Roughly 15 on-genre releases available → ~230 transitions, ~17 h.
- Caveat: digital tracklists sometimes are shorter than the CD tracklist (Chaos in the CBD: 8 unmixed vs. a 77-min mix); verify per release that the unmixed track count ≈ CD track count.

### Gaps
- Bandcamp prices for fabric presents albums could not be fetched (bot challenge); fabric store £8 WAV is for mixed segments only.
- Legacy "fabric NN" / "FABRICLIVE NN" (2001–2018): not systematically checked; they were CD continuous mixes and (per the Kode9 & Burial example) are sold digitally as a continuous-mix file; whether unmixed tracks are bundled for legacy titles is unverified (likely not — they were licensed mixes).
- No listing found for a fabric presents by Nina Kraviz, Maceo Plex, Daniel Avery, Logic1000, Anz, Call Super, Elkka or Dixon in the 2019–2026 Discogs CD catalogue; those names from the brief appear to be wrong (Elkka and HAAi did DJ-Kicks, see Q3).

## Key Question 6 — Licensing context; DJ-mix albums on Bandcamp; "mixed by" discovery tips; academic use of mixed compilations as ground truth

### Takeaway
Licensed mixed compilations are cleared by the label (fabric notes 28 unreleased tracks cleared for Rødhåd; Beatport Mixes required tracks be purchased from Beatport so rights holders got paid); buying them for personal research is ordinary consumer purchase. Academic precedent for mix-to-track alignment exists (Kim et al. ISMIR 2020; Raveform, TISMIR 2026; UnmixDB 2018/19; Sonnleitner 2016), but those works used MixesDB/user tracklists or synthetic mixes rather than commercial "unmixed + CM" releases — which makes the proposed corpus a genuine gap-filler.

### Cited Findings
- Rødhåd's fabric presents featured 28 unreleased tracks (i.e. label-cleared exclusives) — [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/rodhad-announces-fabric-presents-mix-featuring-28-unreleased-tracks-listen)
- Beatport Mixes (Dec 2012): DJs could sell a mix only if it contained tracks purchased from Beatport or their own productions; rights holders paid "as if each track were bought individually"; deals with "tens of thousands of independent labels" — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/beatport-mixes/)
- Raveform (Kim, Kim, Kim & Nam, TISMIR vol. 9, 2026): mix/track metadata from MixesDB ("inherent inaccuracies"); beat-level mix-to-track alignments estimated with Kim et al. (2020); a transition counts when two consecutive identified tracks overlap after alignment; 1,423 tracks manually annotated (beats/downbeats/segments); cites UnmixDB (Schwarz & Fourer 2019, "automatically generated" mixes) and Sonnleitner et al. (2016, 10 mixes/118 tracks); does not use commercial mixed compilations with known source tracks — [TISMIR](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/288)
- Kim et al., "A Computational Analysis of Real-world DJ Mixes Using Mix-To-Track Subsequence Alignment" — [ISMIR 2020](https://program.ismir2020.net/poster_5-18.html); KAIST record — [KAIST](https://koasas.kaist.ac.kr/handle/10203/279066?mode=full)
- UnmixDB: A Dataset for DJ-Mix Information Retrieval (Schwarz & Fourer) — [HAL](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02010431); "Methods and Datasets for DJ-Mix Reverse Engineering" — [HAL](https://hal-univ-evry.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-03184436); "DJ Mix Transcription with Multi-Pass Non-Negative Matrix Factorization" — [arXiv 2410.04198](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.04198)
- Artist-hosted mixes on Bandcamp: the fabric presents model places the full mix + unmixed tracks on the DJ's own Bandcamp (Carlita, Mind Against, Chaos In The CBD, Floorplan, Rødhåd, Amelie Lens examples) — [store.fabriclondon.com](https://store.fabriclondon.com/products/fabric-presents-mind-against); [carlitamusic.bandcamp.com](https://carlitamusic.bandcamp.com/album/fabric-presents-carlita)

### Inferences
- Discovery tips (practical, not sourced): on Bandcamp, search the tags `dj-mix`, `mixed`, `continuous-mix`, `mix-cd` (URL pattern `bandcamp.com/tag/<tag>`), and look for albums whose last track is titled "(Continuous Mix)" / "(DJ Mix)" — the fabric presents, Balance, GU, DJ-Kicks and Late Night Tales releases all follow that naming, and Discogs' dual "Compilation"+"Mixed" format tags are a reliable pre-filter before opening Bandcamp. Many independent DJs also sell mixes of their own productions (fully clearable), which align trivially because the DJ owns every stem — worth a separate pass.
- Because the proposed corpus has *purchasable* unmixed stems for every transition, it offers something Raveform/UnmixDB do not: real human DJ automation with exact source audio, so fader/EQ curves can be recovered by alignment + spectral division rather than estimated.

- Whether buying for ML training (as opposed to listening) is covered by consumer licences is a legal question outside scope; the brief asks for context only.
